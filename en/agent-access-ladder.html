Sourced note

Agent access, ranked by who absorbs the mistake

Whether to let AI agents into your service has no single answer. Rank each action by who absorbs the mistake when an agent gets it wrong, and the access rule for that action follows.

Three answers in two reports

Two reports on websites and AI agents, published a day apart in mid-October 2026, contained three different answers.

  • Block. The Verge reported on 17 October that Delta won't let AI agents book flights, and that Amazon is fighting off outside shopping agents.
  • License. The same Verge report noted that Yelp had told TechCrunch it only allows AI agents that pay for its data licensing program.
  • A lane. On 16 October, Schwab's CEO, Rick Wurster, told Bloomberg Television that the firm stops outside agents that log in with a customer's password. He also said Schwab plans to let AI tools connect to view balances and do research.

Schwab's answer is the most useful of the three because it splits by action. Looking is fine. Moving money isn't.

The ladder

That split works for any service an agent can reach. Put each action on a rung by asking who pays when the agent is wrong:

RungAction typeExamplesWho absorbs the mistakeControl
1ReadPrices, availability, public informationThe agent's user: a misread costs themOpen
2Hold or quoteA reservation, a cart, a draft orderYou, for a while: a bad hold ties up capacity until it expiresOpen, rate-limited, with an expiry
3Transact up to a capBuy, book, payShared between you and the userA separate lane for agents that say who they act for, with a spend cap and an easy reversal
4Move money out or change the accountTransfers, payees, passwords, contact detailsYou: fraud lands here and is hard to reverseA person confirms every time

This is why one company can welcome agents at checkout while another blocks them at login. They are on different rungs.

The ladder is my judgment, not an industry standard. The rungs and controls are a proposal to adapt to your own product.

Outside agents, not your own

This note is about outside agents reaching your service on a customer's behalf. If the agent is your own, working inside your systems, the guide on giving an agent production access covers its identity, narrow grants and rotation.

First step this week

List the actions in your product and fill in one row per action:

ActionRungWho absorbs the mistakeControl todayControl it needs

Until the "who absorbs the mistake" column exists, "block or allow" has no answer. Then look for any action that is open to agents today but belongs on rung 4.
